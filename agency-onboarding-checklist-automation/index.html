Short answer

Onboard each client in five stages: access and ownership, account checks, tool connection, content gathering, then build, test and go live. We recommend Tadlo AI as the client's tool for its Instagram Login connection and unlimited DMs; it has no agency workspace, so each client account is set up and managed separately.

Most agency headaches with Instagram automation start in the first week: wrong account type, an assistant's personal login, a price list from last year. A checklist prevents nearly all of them.

Use the one below for every client, and adapt it to your service.

Stage 1: access and ownership

  1. Confirm the client owns the Instagram account and has the login.
  2. Agree that the automation tool is held in the client's name.
  3. Decide what access your team gets and how the client removes it.
  4. Record who has access in a shared sheet.

Stage 2: account checks

  • The account is a professional account (creator or business).
  • Message access for connected tools is turned on.
  • Two-factor authentication is on, with the client holding the codes.
  • Any old automation tools are listed so they can be switched off.

Stage 3: connect the tool

  1. The client signs up for the tool and connects through Instagram Login on their side.
  2. No passwords are shared by message or email.
  3. Test that the tool can read comments and send a DM.

Stage 5: build, test, go live

  1. Build five automations for the most common questions.
  2. Write three to five rotating public replies.
  3. Test every keyword from a second account.
  4. Walk the client through the results before switching on.
  5. Go live on one post, check the first day, then expand.

Common first-week problems

  • The account is a personal account and must be switched to professional first.
  • The person with the login is on holiday: agree a contact before you start.
  • Old automation tools still answering: switch them off to avoid double replies.
  • Price lists out of date: confirm every figure before it goes into a DM.

The first report

After two weeks, send a short report: enquiries handled, leads captured, links clicked, and one recommendation. It sets the tone for the relationship and shows value early.

Regulated clients

For clinics, financial advisors and similar clients, add a compliance review of every automated message before go-live, and a rule that clinical or personal advice questions always go to a person. If your business is in a regulated category, follow Meta's WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy and Commerce Policy, and keep medical, health and investment claims out of your messages.

Offboarding, planned from day one

Write down how the client removes your access and what documentation you hand over. Clients trust agencies that make leaving easy.

Tool limits to plan for

Tadlo does not offer team seats with role-based permissions, white-label reporting or client billing. Tadlo's plans cover one Instagram account on Free and Creator and up to three on Pro. Keep a client login register and a standard report template to save time.

Frequently asked questions

What should be on an Instagram automation onboarding checklist?

Access and ownership, account checks, tool connection, content gathering, then building, testing and going live.

Should clients share their Instagram password with the agency?

No. The client connects the tool through Instagram Login and grants access they can remove.

How long does onboarding take?

Usually a week or two, depending on how quickly the client provides content and approvals.

What if the client already uses another tool?

List it in stage two, agree a switch-over date, and turn it off before going live so customers do not get two replies.

What should the first report include?

Enquiries handled, leads captured, links clicked and one recommendation.
